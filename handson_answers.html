Exercise 1Read quality (FastQC / MultiQC)

Report: the MultiQC report of your FastQC result.

Q1.1Which, if any, samples are overrepresented?full data
None stands far above the rest; S5_diff is clearly under-represented. In the full run S2, S4 and S6 got about 22.6–22.8 million reads each, S1 17.9 M, S3 14.6 M, but S5 only 4.3 M (a quarter of the mean, 17.5 M). Your course data were cut to exactly 100,000 reads per sample, so your MultiQC shows six equal bars.

Bars: read counts of the full sequencing run (dataset table of the course data, column Read Count).

Q1.2Are there differences in base quality scores across or within the samples?course data (p1100)
Quality is good in all six samples. The mean Phred score stays above 30 for most of the read and falls slowly towards the end of the 151-base read (to 27–29 at the last base). Across samples the curves are almost the same, except S2_undiff, which drops earlier, to about 24 around base 135. Within each sample the pattern is the usual one: a few lower bases at the very start, high in the middle, lower at the end.

Green zone: Phred ≥ 28 (an error rate below about 1 in 600). The one curve that dips into the orange zone is S2_undiff.

Q1.3Is there any adapter contamination in any of the samples? What might be the cause of this?course data (p1100)
Yes, in all six (FastQC flags adapter content as failed for every sample). It is strongest in S2_undiff and S5_diff: more than 5 % of their reads already contain adapter at base 16, and about 94 % by the end of the read. Cause: the cDNA fragment (the insert) is shorter than the 151-base read, so the sequencer reads past it into the adapter, and then into nothing, which a NextSeq records as a run of G.

Q1.4What would be a good preprocessing strategy for this data?course data (p1100)
Trim adapters and poly-A/poly-G tails, trim the low-quality end of the read, and drop reads that become too short. Optionally cut the first few bases (FastQC's per-base sequence content fails at the start of every sample, the usual random-priming bias); that is the trim_front1 = 4 suggested in Exercise 2. In Omics-Studio, STAR runs this preprocessing (fastp) before mapping.

Exercise 2Mapping and IGV: the FST gene

The questions use the full-read BAM files linked in the exercise (your own BAMs have too few reads for one gene).

Q2.1What is the gene model? Do our reads map to the right part of the gene?full and course data
FST lies on chromosome 5 (53,480,626–53,487,134, + strand) and has 6 exons. Yes: the reads pile up on the exons, and split reads (thin lines in IGV) jump from one exon to the next. Almost nothing maps to the introns.

IGV, chr5:53,478,339–53,489,134. Upper two tracks: course data (100,000 reads); lower two: full data.

Q2.2Is the gene expressed in samples from both conditions?full data
Yes, but at very different levels. In the full data FST has 430–810 reads per million uniquely mapped reads in the undifferentiated samples and 16–20 in the differentiated ones. In the 100,000-read course BAMs the diff samples have almost no FST reads, which can look like "not expressed"; the full BAMs still have 46–405.

Counted with samtools on the full-read STAR BAMs (uniquely mapped reads in the FST locus), divided by each sample's uniquely mapped reads.

Q2.3Is there a difference in the read coverage between samples from both conditions?full and course data
Yes, about 20–50 times more in undiff. S1_undiff has 806 FST reads per million, S4_diff 20; the IGV coverage scales show the same (0–3,999 against 0–145). edgeR on the course data agrees: FST log2 ratio −2.34 (diff over undiff), FDR 8 × 10⁻¹⁰.

With only 100,000 reads the course data underestimate the difference: a gene with 0–3 reads in diff cannot show a 40-fold change.

Q2.4How many isoforms are identified?full data
Two main isoforms, which differ in their last exon. Exon 5 is joined either to a last exon starting at 53,485,951 (FST-201; 538 split reads in S1_undiff) or to one starting 264 bases earlier, at 53,485,687 (FST-202; 63 split reads). Exon 5 also has two acceptor sites 3 bases apart (707 and 218 reads, drawn as one arc): a small variant of the same exon, not a different isoform.

Drawn from the split reads in the full BAMs; the Sashimi plot below it is the one in the exercise (same samples, slightly different counts because of a different mapping run).

Q2.5Which isoforms are present almost exclusively in one sample? Which sample?full data
The FST-202 ending (exon 5 joined to the last exon at 53,485,687) is seen in S1_undiff (63 split reads) and not in S4_diff (0). Of the two samples loaded in the exercise it belongs to the undifferentiated one; the other undiff samples have it too (S2 15, S3 25), no diff sample has it.

In the figure above: the thin arc into the grey part of exon 6 exists only above the line (S1).

Q3.3How best to proceed with underrepresented libraries? Generate more reads, or exclude them?course data (p1100)
It depends on why the library is small. If the library is good but got fewer reads (S5_diff in the full run: 4.3 M), sequence it again: with three replicates per group, losing one costs a lot of power. Exclude a sample only if its quality is poor and it behaves as an outlier in clustering. S5 still clusters with the diff samples (Q3.4), so here: keep it, and ideally add reads.
Q3.4How do the samples cluster in the dendrogram?course data (p1100)
By condition: S4, S5, S6 (diff) form one branch and S1, S2, S3 (undiff) the other. Within the branches S4 and S6 are closest, and S1 and S3; S5 and S2 join their group last.
Q3.5Are there any outliers? Would you retain or exclude these from downstream analysis?course data (p1100)
No real outliers. S2_undiff and S5_diff are the least similar members of their groups (they are the two short-insert libraries), but each still clusters with its own condition. Keep them.

Exercise 4Differential expression (edgeR)

Report: the Static Report of your EdgeR_diff--over--undiff result.

Q4.1How many genes have p-value < 0.01 and a log2 fold change of ≥ ±2?course data (p1100)
71 genes (row p < 0.01, column fc ≥ 4: a log2 fold change of 2 is a 4-fold change). 62 of them are higher in diff, 9 higher in undiff.

"fc" in this table is the linear fold change in either direction: fc ≥ 2 means log2 ≥ 1, fc ≥ 4 means log2 ≥ 2.

Q4.2Look at genes with log2 fold change ≥ 1, what happens to the number of DEGs with decreasing p-value?course data (p1100)
It falls, but slowly: 293 at p < 0.05, 288 at p < 0.01, 245 at p < 0.001, 158 at p < 10⁻⁵. Genes with large fold changes tend to have very small p-values too, so a stricter p-value removes only some of them.

Values: column fc ≥ 2 of the result summary above.

Q4.3What does the volcano plot show us in terms of distribution of our DEGs between our two conditions?course data (p1100)
It is lopsided towards diff: more genes, and larger changes, on the right (higher in diff). At p ≤ 0.01: 263 genes higher in diff, 160 higher in undiff. The strongest increases reach a log2 ratio of about 7 (SERPINA3 7.07), the strongest decreases only about −2.8 (KRT14 −2.76, NDRG1 −2.74).

Light blue: significant genes. Points on the top edge and at the right edge are capped (−log10 p at 10, log2 ratio at 5) – SERPINA3, LCN2 and others lie beyond the plot.
